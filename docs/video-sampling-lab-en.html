MODELATLAS · ORIGINAL SYNTHETIC LESSON / TEMPORAL EVIDENCE

Video sampling lab: observed is not continuous

Prerequisites: timestamps and the distinction between image and video inputs. Outcome: select a sampling stride and offset, locate a middle-frame identity discontinuity, and report both observed facts and what remains unproved.

The nine-frame sequences, identity labels and coordinates are original supplied fixtures—not model-generated video, real tracking results or a physics simulation. No generation model, API, VBench, real benchmark, network requests or progress storage. This demonstrates missed evidence under finite sampling, not vendor quality.

1 · Mechanism: which frames are selected?

Frame index i = 0…8, timestamp t = 250i milliseconds, position x = 10 + 10i in teaching units. Each frame contains one identity-labelled block. Offset o and stride s select i = o + ks for nonnegative integers k and i ≤ 8. Here s counts frames, not seconds; 0 ≤ o < s.

Identity consistency and smooth positions are different checks. Increasing positions do not prove stable identity. No observed change means only no change in the selected frames—not in omitted frames or between timestamps. Even all nine supplied frames cannot establish continuous motion at times not supplied.

2 · Worked example: same endpoints, different middle

Sequence W starts and ends with A. With s=4, o=0, F0/F4/F8 are all A; F3 (750 ms, B, x=40) is missed. With s=4, o=3, F3/F7 reveal B→A. Then s=1, o=0 inspects all nine frames: A at F2 → B at F3 → A at F4. Positions remain regular despite the identity discontinuity.

    5 · Transfer and limits

    For a real video, first state the target property (identity, position or action), time coverage and sampling rule, then report gaps. “No problem sampled” is not “no problem exists.” Real identity recognition also faces occlusion, viewpoints and recognition errors; this lesson supplies labels and implements no recognition.
